# Citations Guidance

## Cite the technical claim

Place citations close to the claim they support.

## Group citations only when they support the same statement

A citation cluster should represent one common research fact, not unrelated papers.

## Use author-prominent citations for attribution

Use when identifying:
- a closest study;
- origin of an algorithm/mechanism;
- theorem/property source.

## Use information-prominent citations for synthesis

Use when several papers support a background or research-stream statement.

## Comparison sentence patterns

> `Both A and B consider [common feature], but they differ in [dimension].`

> `Building on [base setting], C additionally incorporates [feature].`

> `The setting of A is closest to the present problem because ...; however, it assumes ..., whereas the present study considers ...`

> `Taken together, these studies establish ..., but they do not address ...`

## Novelty wording

Do not infer `no study has considered...` from a limited search.

Use wording proportional to verification coverage:
- `The reviewed studies do not jointly consider ...`
- `To the best of our knowledge, ...` only with adequate search coverage.

## Prefer primary sources

Use original papers for:
- problem definitions;
- algorithm attribution;
- benchmark generation;
- theorem/property;
- experimental result.

Use reviews for field classification and broad background.
