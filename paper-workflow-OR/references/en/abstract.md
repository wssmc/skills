# Abstract

Write the abstract as a self-contained miniature paper addressing five questions in sequence:

```text
Research background → Research problem → Method → Main results → Implications
```

## Generic structure

### 1. Background and problem

One to two sentences positioning the research field and the specific gap.

Common patterns:

```text
[Research topic] plays an important role in [application or research field].
However, existing studies have not adequately addressed [specific problem].
```

Do not write a lengthy industry review or accumulate macro-level significance.

### 2. Objective

State what this study investigates, solves, or tests.

Common patterns:

```text
This study investigates ...
This paper addresses ...
The objective of this study is to ...
```

The research object, core variables, or problem name must appear explicitly here.

### 3. Method

Summarize the data, model, experimental design, or solution approach. Include only what defines the contribution; omit parameters and implementation details.

Common patterns:

```text
To address this problem, we develop/propose/formulate ...
The proposed framework consists of ...
The method is evaluated using ...
```

The method block typically answers:

* where the data come from;
* what model or framework was built;
* what analytical or solution method was used;
* how validation was conducted.

### 4. Main results

The most important part of the abstract. Report the central finding with quantitative results, comparisons, or statistical conclusions when possible.

Weak:

```text
The results show that the proposed method is effective.
```

Better:

```text
The proposed method reduced the average error by 12.4% and
outperformed the strongest baseline on 38 of 45 test cases.
```

Results must be consistent with the full manuscript — do not report numbers that do not appear in the main text.

### 5. Implications

One to two sentences on what the results imply, what problem was solved, and the theoretical or practical value.

Common patterns:

```text
These findings demonstrate that ...
The study provides ...
The proposed framework offers an effective approach for ...
```

Do not reintroduce the method or claim conclusions not supported by the manuscript.

## Generic English template

```latex
[Topic or practical context] is important for [research field or
application]. However, existing studies have not sufficiently addressed
[specific research gap]. This study investigates [research objective or
problem]. To address this problem, we develop/propose [main model,
framework, or method] and evaluate it using [data, experiments, cases,
or benchmarks]. The results show that [main quantitative or qualitative
finding]. In particular, [most important comparison, mechanism, or
secondary finding]. These findings demonstrate that [main implication]
and provide [theoretical or practical contribution].
```

## Emphasis by paper type

### Empirical study

```text
Background → Research question → Data and sample → Analytical method → Main findings → Theoretical or managerial implications
```

### Algorithm and optimization study

```text
Application problem → Mathematical problem definition → Model and algorithm → Experimental setup → Performance results → Method value
```

### Theoretical study

```text
Theoretical background → Open problem → Core assumptions or model → Derivation method → Theorem or conclusion → Theoretical significance
```

### Survey paper

```text
Research field → Scope of review → Literature screening method → Classification framework → Main trends and gaps → Future directions
```

## Writing principles

A good abstract should:

* be self-contained — readable without the main text;
* be a single paragraph (unless the journal uses structured abstracts);
* contain no citations, figures, tables, or complex equations;
* define every abbreviation on first use;
* summarize the method without unpacking it;
* report specific results without enumerating every finding;
* avoid numbers or claims that do not appear in the manuscript;
* avoid bare evaluation words such as `novel`, `significant`, or `effective` without evidence;
* avoid concatenating the Introduction and Conclusion as a substitute for a coherent abstract.

## Length guideline

For an approximately 200-word abstract:

| Component | Approximate proportion |
|---|---|
| Background and gap | 15%–20% |
| Objective | 10%–15% |
| Method | 25%–30% |
| Results | 30%–40% |
| Implications | 10%–15% |

The most common faults are **excessive background, overly detailed method, and too few results**. A strong abstract allocates the largest share to results.
