# Conclusion Writing Specification

## 1. Reconnect the problem and contribution without restarting the paper

Begin from what the paper solved, not from generic industry background.

Summarize:
- studied problem;
- formulation;
- principal method contribution.

## 2. Select findings by scientific importance

Prioritize:
- strongest overall performance result;
- exact/MIP evidence when available;
- statistical support;
- mechanism/component finding;
- important boundary or reversal.

Do not repeat Chapter 5 in table order.

## 3. Distinguish performance findings from mechanism findings

When Component Analysis exists, retain at least one mechanism-level finding if it changes understanding of why the method works.

## 4. Write limitations as scope boundaries

A limitation should state:
- what is excluded;
- why it matters;
- how it bounds the conclusion.

Avoid generic `more complex real-world factors remain` statements.

## 5. Derive future work from the limitation mechanism

Use:

```text
current limitation
→ why it matters
→ corresponding future extension
```

Do not list unrelated dynamic, multi-objective, energy, human-resource, or uncertainty extensions simply because they are common future-work topics.

## 6. End with the strongest supported implication

Do not claim universal industrial applicability unless deployment evidence exists.
